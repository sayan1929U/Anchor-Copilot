According to Deloitte's 2026 Global Gen Z and Millennial Survey, "not being recognized enough" is identified
as the second leading cause of burnout among Gen Z and millennial employees - not workload, as commonly
assumed.

The survey's broader well-being findings show some encouraging trends: more employees now believe their
organizations take mental health seriously, and confidence in manager support and workplace psychological
safety has improved compared to previous years. Still, burnout remains deeply embedded in workplace culture
and is one of the most cited reasons employees hesitate to pursue leadership roles.
