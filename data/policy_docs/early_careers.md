# Early Careers & Onboarding Guide

New employees complete a structured 2-week onboarding program covering company systems, team introductions,
and role-specific training, followed by a 30/60/90-day check-in cycle with their manager to track progress
and address any early concerns.

Every new hire is assigned an onboarding buddy for their first month, available for informal "no such thing
as a dumb question" questions that don't need to go through a manager.

Early-career employees have access to a dedicated Slack channel specifically for onboarding questions about
company norms, tools, and processes, monitored by both HR and experienced peers who volunteer to help.

The company runs a formal Graduate/Early Careers Program with structured rotations across 2-3 teams during
the first year, intended to help early-career employees find the function that best fits their interests
before settling into a permanent team.

New hires are given a "First 90 Days" guide covering not just tasks but unwritten culture norms - how
meetings typically run, how feedback is usually given, and who to ask about what - to reduce the anxiety
of learning things by trial and error.

It is explicitly normal and expected to feel uncertain in the first few months; managers are trained not
to expect full productivity from new hires until at least the 90-day mark, and early performance
conversations are framed around learning progress, not output volume.

Early-career employees can request additional 1:1 time with their manager beyond the standard cadence
during their first 6 months - this is encouraged, not seen as needing "extra help."
